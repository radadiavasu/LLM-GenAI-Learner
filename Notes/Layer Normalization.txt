Layer Normalization in Transformers


Q. What is Normalization & Why Do We Need It?

In deep learning, normalization transforms data so it stays within a predictable range (e.g., mean of 0 and standard deviation of 1).


Q. Why Do We Need It?

Ans: 1. Stabilizes Training: Keeps extreme numbers from blowing up, preventing exploding/vanishing gradients.

     2. Faster Convergence: Helps the model learn much quicker.

     3. Prevents Internal Covariate Shift: Stops the inner layers of a neural network from panicking when the distribution of inputs keeps shifting during training.


* Batch Normalization vs. Layer Normalization (The Core Difference)

The biggest difference between the two is the direction in which they calculate the mean ($\mu$) and standard deviation ($\sigma$):


1. Batch Normalization (The Column Approach):

Normalizes across the batch (vertically down columns of rows).

Analogy: Grading a whole classroom of students on a curve together.

2. Layer Normalization (The Row Approach):

Normalizes across the features/dimensions (horizontally across a single token/row).

Analogy: Grading each student individually based on their own test answers.


Q. Why Batch Normalization FAILS in Transformers (The Padding Problem)?

Ans: For example,

Imagine you have a batch of sentences. One sentence has 100 words, but another sentence has only 2 words and is padded with 98 zeros.

If you use Batch Normalization, you compute statistics vertically down columns. Those columns get flooded with hundreds of unneeded padding zeros.

The Result: The mean and standard deviation become completely skewed and artificial. They do not represent the true text data, causing the model to break down.


Q. Why Layer Normalization Wins in Transformers?

Ans: When processing the word "bank" in a sentence:

1. It looks at its embedding dimensions (e.g., dimensions 1, 2, and 3).

2. It calculates the mean and standard deviation strictly for that specific word.

3. In result the padding zeros in other rows have zero impact on how this token is normalized. The math remains clean, pure, and accurate.
